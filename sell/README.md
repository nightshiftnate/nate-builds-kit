# Price and list your stuff with ChatGPT or Claude

From the "ChatGPT can now help you sell things" carousel.

ChatGPT won't post the listing for you. It can tell you what an item is, look up what it sells for used and write the listing. You still post it yourself.

## 1. Snap it and ask what it is

Take a few clear photos: the front, the back, any labels or model numbers, and any damage.

Upload them to ChatGPT and ask:

> What is this, and what does it usually sell for used?

Image upload works on Free and paid ChatGPT plans, with usage limits that depend on the plan.

Claude reads photos too. Click the **+** button in the lower left of the chat box and add them.

## 2. Ask for a current price

Ask it to search the web:

> Search for what this exact item has sold for recently. Show me your sources.

ChatGPT search is on every plan, Free included.

## 3. Check the sources yourself

OpenAI's own help page says search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect. Open the links it gives you. Check the dates, and check that it's the same model and condition, before you set a price.

## 4. Have it write the listing

> Write a short eBay title and a description for this. List every flaw you can see in the photos.

Read it over and fix anything it got wrong. Make sure the listing matches the photos.

## 5. Or let eBay's app do the listing part

On 11 November 2026 eBay announced a camera-first way to list in its app: you take photos, and eBay identifies the item and fills in the listing details. You review it and hit publish yourself. eBay's words: "Nothing goes live without your approval." At launch it covered Australia, France, Germany, Italy, the UK and the US.

## 6. On Facebook Marketplace, never ship off a screenshot

Buyers sometimes send a screenshot that looks like a payment. Facebook's help page says: "You should never trust screenshots of payments as confirmation." Check that the money is really in your account before you ship.
